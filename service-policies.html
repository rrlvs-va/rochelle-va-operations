Purpose

Starting or running a business can feel messy, especially when you are still figuring out what to organize, what to delegate, and what should be handled first.

These service policies explain how I work with clients, especially people who are new to hiring a VA (Virtual Assistant) or operations support. They are meant to make expectations clear before work starts.

A proposal, written agreement, or agreed message thread may include more specific details for a particular project. If something is important, it should be written down before we rely on it.

In simple terms: this page explains what I can help with, what I need from you, and where the limits are so we do not work from assumptions.

A few terms I may use

I try to keep things simple, but a few work terms may still come up. On this page, this is what I mean:

VA means Virtual Assistant — support for admin, communication, organization, research, follow-ups, and similar work.

CRM means Customer Relationship Management system — a tool for tracking customers, leads, notes, follow-ups, or deals.

SOP means Standard Operating Procedure — a written step-by-step guide for doing a task.

Scope means the work we agreed is included.

Out of scope means work that is not included unless we agree to add it.

Turnaround time means how long the task is expected to take.

Rush work means work needed faster than the normal agreed timeline.

Final approval means your confirmation that something is ready to send, post, publish, submit, or use.

What I can help with

I can help with practical support work that keeps your business moving. This may include admin support, scheduling, inbox help, file organization, research, note-taking, task tracking, follow-ups, customer or client messages, CRM updates, simple trackers, SOPs, basic reports, and other support tasks we agree on.

I can also help with workflow cleanup, which simply means making the work easier to track, repeat, organize, or hand off. This may include checklists, trackers, simple process notes, folder organization, status updates, and clearer task flows.

Creative and content support may include presentations, simple graphics, document formatting, light branding support, social content support, image editing, short-form video support, portfolio materials, and other supporting assets when they fit the work requested.

In simple terms: I help organize the work, track what needs to happen, support communication, and keep things from falling through the cracks.

What I need from you

To do the work properly, I need clear instructions, necessary files, deadlines, priorities, access to the right tools, and timely feedback.

If the work involves your brand, customers, offers, pricing, refunds, products, services, or public claims, I need you to provide or approve the correct information.

You do not need to have everything perfectly organized before asking for help. Part of the work may be helping sort through what is scattered or unclear. But if a request is too vague, risky, missing key details, or not ready to start, I may need to ask questions first.

In simple terms: I can do better work when I know what you need, when you need it, and what rules or details I should follow.

Boundaries

I can help you organize information, compare options, prepare notes, draft materials, build checklists, and make things easier to review.

I cannot be the owner of your business for you. Final decisions about pricing, offers, contracts, refunds, hiring, taxes, legal requirements, customer promises, business strategy, and anything that affects your money, legal responsibilities, or reputation remain your responsibility.

If you are unsure what to do, I can help you lay out the information more clearly. For decisions that require professional advice, you may need to speak with the right licensed professional.

In simple terms: I can help you see things more clearly, but I cannot make owner-level decisions for you.

Approval before use

I can help draft, prepare, organize, format, schedule, or review materials. You are responsible for final approval before something is sent, posted, published, submitted, promised, or used publicly under your business name.

This includes emails, newsletters, social media posts, offers, prices, refunds, customer messages, ads, testimonials, website content, public announcements, forms, scripts, proposals, and other materials that represent your business.

If you ask me to send, publish, or use something without review, I may still ask for written approval first when the material involves public claims, money, customer commitments, legal wording, sensitive information, or anything that could affect your business reputation.

In simple terms: I can prepare the work, but you need to approve anything that represents your business before it goes out.

What I do not provide

I do not provide legal, tax, accounting, medical, financial, insurance, immigration, real-estate, or other licensed professional advice. I can help organize information or prepare admin materials, but the final professional review should come from someone qualified to give it.

I do not knowingly help with fake reviews, fake testimonials, misleading claims, impersonation, unauthorized access, deceptive outreach, plagiarism, misuse of private information, or anything that breaks laws, platform rules, privacy rules, or client-approved processes.

I also cannot guarantee sales, followers, leads, engagement, approvals, conversions, customer decisions, platform results, search ranking, business growth, or outcomes controlled by other people, platforms, markets, or systems.

In simple terms: I can support your business, but I do not replace licensed professionals, help with dishonest work, or promise results I cannot control.

Changes, timing & payments

Sometimes a task sounds simple at first but becomes bigger once we start. For example, a simple flyer may turn into copywriting, image selection, layout, revisions, caption writing, and formatting for different platforms.

If the work grows beyond what we first agreed, we may need to adjust the deadline, price, or priority list. Adding more work may mean adding more time or moving other tasks later.

Rates, payment timing, currency, billing method, and payment channel should be agreed before work begins. Payment account details are shared privately after the work setup is confirmed. Work may pause or be delayed if payment, onboarding, access, or approvals are not completed on time.

In simple terms: more work, late feedback, missing access, or a change in direction can affect timing, cost, and what gets done first.

Access & passwords

If I need to work inside your email, CRM, calendar, cloud folder, project tool, website, social account, or other system, please give access through the proper method when possible.

Please do not send banking passwords, one-time passwords, full payment-card details, or private logins I do not need. If a platform allows you to add me as a user with limited access, that is usually better than sharing a main password.

If I do not have the access needed for a task, or if access is removed or changed, the work may be delayed or paused.

In simple terms: give me the access needed to do the job, but do not send sensitive information casually.

Reviews & portfolio

If you choose to give a rating, review, recommendation, or testimonial, I may use it as business proof, portfolio material, or marketing content.

I may lightly format feedback so it fits my website or portfolio, but I will not intentionally change the meaning. If identifying details need to be limited, please say so before publication.

I do not intentionally publish private client information, confidential work, private messages, screenshots of non-public communications, or identifiable client materials without appropriate permission.

In simple terms: I may share feedback you choose to give me, but I will not expose private client work or private conversations without permission.

Privacy note

Privacy, client data, AI-assisted tools, call or meeting recordings, social media interactions, external platforms, and payment-related information are addressed in my Privacy Policy.

This Service Policies page explains working expectations. The Privacy Policy explains how information may be handled.

In simple terms: service boundaries are here. Privacy details are in the Privacy Policy.
